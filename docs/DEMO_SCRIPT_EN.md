# FailFirst AI — 3-minute English demo voiceover

**Target:** up to 3 minutes, with pauses for screen transitions. AI results vary; the quote figures match one observed run. Recheck the recording before submitting.

---

Business decisions can look profitable, until one hidden assumption turns them into losses.

This is FailFirst AI, built during the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon. It stress-tests business decisions before money is committed.

There are eight modes: contracts, subscriptions, hiring, inventory, discounts, advertising, new services, and new branches.

Let's test an eight-thousand-dollar contract. The baseline profit is two thousand dollars. But twenty extra labor hours and ten percent material inflation leave just one hundred sixty dollars in profit. That's a two-percent margin.

Now I ask Meta Muse Spark to find overlooked risks. The model reviews the context and calls a bounded simulation tool. The financial calculations run in JavaScript on the server, not inside the language model.

In this example, Muse Spark tests additional weekend work and excluded delivery. Under those hypothetical costs, the remaining profit becomes a six-hundred-ninety-dollar loss.

The break-even quote increases to eight thousand six hundred ninety dollars. To target an illustrative fifteen-percent margin under this scenario, the price would need to be about ten thousand two hundred twenty-four dollars.

These are not predicted costs. FailFirst identifies what to verify, including delivery charges, overtime rates, and inspection obligations, before signing.

Next is Subscription Launch. We model customer acquisition, churn, revenue, and operating expenses over six months. A positive cumulative result can hide an unprofitable final month. And launching from zero customers is different from starting with existing subscribers.

In Bulk versus Small Batch, the calculator includes unsold stock and salvage assumptions. It finds the demand threshold where buying in bulk becomes more profitable. This is a decision flip, not a sales forecast.

Under the hood, Meta generates risk hypotheses and selects bounded tools. Deterministic code recalculates the scenarios. We also run sixty-four automated calculation tests.

Meta proposes the risks. Code checks the numbers. The user decides with better evidence.

FailFirst AI. Break the plan. Not the business.
